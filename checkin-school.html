HISTI for Schools

Ages 10–18 · Optional class activity

Who will you imagine?

Start with one person: an unfamiliar student, a classmate, a friend, or a trusted adult. No names needed. Each person has 48 questions across five parts; you can explore others later or skip a person.

Allow about 35–50 minutes for the class activity, including directions and reflection. Some students need longer. This is not a timed test.

No answer is good or bad.

A higher openness score is not a better grade, a sign of honesty, or an instruction to share. Participation is optional. You can skip any question or person, stop, or ask for a different activity.

How it stays private ↗
For teachers and caregivers

This is an optional classroom reflection for ages 10–18, not a test of character, behavior, health, honesty, or safety. The school adaptation has not been validated as equivalent to the full instrument.

  • Offer a non-disclosure alternative activity. Do not require all questions or all four people.
  • Do not collect named responses or ask students to explain private choices aloud. Never ask them to identify a classmate, friend, caregiver, or teacher.
  • Do not request real private facts or adult signatures. For a graded lesson, assess an imaginary-example reflection or understanding of boundaries, not individual scores.
  • If used for research, obtain the relevant school review, consent/assent, data-protection, and safeguarding approvals before collecting responses.
  • A willingness rating is not advice to share. A trusted adult can be an appropriate source of help; safety can also mean choosing not to share.
  • If a student independently asks for help, use ordinary school safeguarding procedures. Do not infer abuse, health issues, or risk from ratings.
  • On shared devices, let students reset their local check-in when finished. Nothing on this page collects or uploads student responses.

Optional reflection using imaginary examples: Why might someone share differently with a friend and an unfamiliar student? How is a general fact different from a precise private detail? What is one polite way to set a boundary?
